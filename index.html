PEPCEI: Pulmonary Embolism Prediction using CXR and EKG Imaging

Leveraging AI to improve early diagnosis of Pulmonary Embolism

Welcome to the PEPCEI Project

A. The Clinical Problem

A pulmonary embolism (PE) occurs when a blood clot gets stuck in an artery in the lung, blocking blood flow to the lungs. Early detection and treatment are critical to reduce the risk of serious complications and death. The current diagnostic gold standard, the CT pulmonary angiogram (CTPA), is effective but exposes people to radiation, contrast, and increased cost.

B. The Proposed Solution and Overall Vision

This project aims to address these challenges through a systematic investigation of machine learning (ML) models capable of predicting PE from more readily available and safer data sources. By developing a predictive tool that leverages data from routine Chest X-Rays (CXR) and Electrocardiograms (EKG), we aim to create a powerful, low-cost screening and diagnostic aid. The overarching vision is to enhance early PE detection, thereby reducing morbidity and mortality in our patient populations. A successful model would simultaneously lessen the reliance on expensive CT scans, providing a safer and more efficient diagnostic pathway. This entire study is based on the publicly available MIMIC-IV dataset.

C. Initial Technical Approach: Embeddings and CNNs

Our technical approach begins with an initial phase focused on pre-computed embeddings to establish a strong performance baseline. For the radiography component, we will utilize the 4,096-dimensional feature vectors from the PhysioNet MIMIC-CXR dataset, which were generated by a state-of-the-art EfficientNet-L2 foundation model. This allows us to leverage deep representations of image abnormalities without the computational overhead of image processing. These rich CXR vectors will be combined with corresponding embeddings derived from EKG waveform data, and the initial models developed to fuse and interpret this data will be based on Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures.

D. Advanced and Future Directions: Raw Data and Transformers

Building upon this foundation, the subsequent phase will engage directly with the raw data modalities. A multi-modal CNN architecture will be developed to process both the 2D pixel data from chest X-rays and the 1D time-series signals from electrocardiograms, allowing the model to learn features end-to-end. Looking forward, the project’s ultimate goal is to advance beyond conventional CNNs and explore the capabilities of transformer models.

Key Objectives

  • Develop Novel Models: Creating state-of-the-art CNN and Transformer-based models for PE detection in CXRs.
  • Curate Datasets: Assembling and meticulously annotating a large-scale CXR and EKG dataset.
  • Validate Rigorously: Ensuring our models are robust, fair, and generalizable through extensive validation.
  • Promote Open Science: Sharing our findings, code (where appropriate), and insights with the research community.
  • Facilitate Clinical Translation: Exploring pathways to integrate our tools into clinical workflows to aid decision-making.

Follow our progress through publications, presentations, and updates on this website. We aim to make significant contributions to improving patient outcomes for PE.
